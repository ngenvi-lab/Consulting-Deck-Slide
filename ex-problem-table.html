WHAT LOOKS ABNORMAL

1

Stock rises while payables fall

Dec inventory +0.8bn while payables owed fall 3.7bn. A normal stock-up is supplier-funded: in Oct, stock +0.6bn, payables +0.7bn.

2

Working capital triples

3.0bn in Oct to 9.0bn in Dec (27 → 75 days); last year it fell to 1.9bn (24 days).

Source: [report / table], management basis, USD bn, as of [date]; A = actual, F = forecast. Working capital = receivables + inventory + payables (payables negative). Days: receivables ÷ revenue, others ÷ COGS, × 365/12. Definitions: appendix.

Headline: in the Q4 forecast, stock rises while payables fall, and December working capital triples. On the left, blue columns are actuals and orange columns are forecast. Payables are shown negative because they are money we owe, and the rows add up to working capital. The bottom block shows the same months last year, so we can rule out seasonality. Point 1 only shows when inventory and payables are read together: rising stock alone can be a normal stock-up, but a normal stock-up is funded by suppliers. In the December forecast stock still rises while payables fall by 3.7bn. Point 2: as a result working capital triples to 9.0bn, 75 days. Last year the same months went the other way.
